NJIT SIGCHI Design Jam: funnymoney

User Experience Hackathon Project

Role Design Lead
Timeline 10 Hours
Tools Google Forms, Google Sheets, FigJam, Figma
UX Research UX Design Prototyping Hackathon

Project Overview

funnymoney was created by Team UWAWA during the NJIT SIGCHI Design Jam. In a 10-hour sprint, we moved from research to a testable prototype concept focused on how young adults budget for leisure without feeling overwhelmed.

As Design Lead, I helped shape the research direction, synthesize insights, and guide our interface decisions from early ideation through paper prototyping and user testing.

Research Snapshot

42.9%

Reported being somewhat confident in financial planning.

57.1%

Reported feeling neutral or not confident at all about financial planning.

81%

Check their balance instead of following a specific budgeting rule.

35.7%

Consider budget first when planning leisure activities.

The Challenge

Our problem statement: users often do not budget their finances for leisure. Anxiety and avoidance around financial management, combined with everyday "adulting" responsibilities, causes leisure activities to be deprioritized and can contribute to burnout.

We needed to design an experience that supports financial awareness without adding more stress, while helping users still make room for hobbies and social activities.

The Solution

We designed funnymoney, a budgeting concept tailored specifically to leisure spending. The app helps users set and monitor a fun budget, validate purchases, and find affordable activities based on their available balance.

Key concept features included a savings progress bar, budget method manager, "fun money" tracker, purchase validation prompts, an activity finder, and saved/liked events.

Process

We used a compressed mixed-method process to make evidence-based decisions within the 10-hour design jam window:

  • Research Framing: Defined three guiding questions around financial habits, engagement features, and how social anxiety intersects with budget constraints during leisure planning.
  • Primary + Secondary Research: Ran a survey with 42 participants, conducted 2 semi-structured interviews, and reviewed budgeting frameworks (including 50/30/20 and zero-based budgeting).
  • Synthesis: Used Google Sheets and affinity diagrams to identify behavior patterns and user needs.
  • Ideation: Rapidly explored concepts in FigJam and prioritized feasible features for a hackathon prototype scope.
  • Prototyping: Created paper prototypes, then translated the direction into Figma visuals (WIP at presentation time).
  • User Testing: Tested the dashboard paper prototype and iterated on clarity, especially around the "Fun Money" widget.

As Design Lead, I kept the team aligned on user-centered priorities, balanced feasibility with ambition, and drove design decisions across each sprint phase.

Outcomes

By the end of the Design Jam, our team delivered a research-backed concept and prototype direction that connected budgeting with leisure planning in a way that felt practical and approachable.

Key outcomes:

  • Identified a clear opportunity: many users track spending informally, with low confidence in structured financial planning.
  • Validated dashboard intuitiveness in paper prototype testing, while uncovering and improving ambiguity around the "Fun Money" metric.
  • Defined a feasible future roadmap, including support for cash spending, calendar/event matching, and gamified milestones.

What I learned: strong hackathon design leadership means grounding fast decisions in real user signals, keeping scope focused, and turning early testing feedback into immediate, high-impact iteration.
