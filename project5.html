Laptop Stickers as Self-Expression

Exploring how everyday artifacts become curated identity signals in public campus spaces

Role UX Researcher (Independent Project)
Timeline 1 Month
Tools Google Forms, Apple Voice Memos, Microsoft Word, Qualitative Coding
UX Research Qualitative Analysis Visual Ethnography Thematic Analysis Identity Research Affinity Mapping

Project Overview

This mixed-methods study examined how NJIT students use laptop stickers as tools for identity expression, memory preservation, and social signaling in public academic spaces.

Through interviews, visual ethnography, and open-ended questionnaires, I explored how sticker choice, placement, and evolution reflect students' values, emotional attachments, and management of public perception.

The study positions laptop stickers as more than decoration — they function as curated personal artifacts that mediate identity and belonging on campus.

The Challenge

Laptop stickers are extremely common among college students but largely understudied. While they're visible everywhere on campus, little research examines why students choose specific stickers, how they arrange them, or what these choices reveal about identity construction in academic spaces.

This project aimed to explore the deeper meanings behind what might appear to be casual decoration, uncovering the intentional curation and social negotiation happening through this everyday practice.

Research Methodology

I designed a mixed-methods qualitative study combining three research approaches:

  • Visual ethnography — 13 participants
  • Semi-structured interviews — 3 participants
  • Open-ended questionnaire responses — Additional contextual data

Through multi-cycle qualitative coding (In Vivo, Versus, Values coding) and affinity mapping, I identified patterns across participant experiences and organized them into key thematic categories:

Personal Meaning & Sentimental Value

How emotional attachments drive sticker selection

Curated Identity & Expression

Managing public self-presentation through stickers

Aesthetics & Organization

Spatial logic and visual arrangement patterns

Social Interaction & Belonging

Stickers as conversation starters and signals

Change Over Time

How sticker collections evolve with identity

This structured synthesis transformed informal personal decoration into analyzable behavioral patterns.

Key Findings

1. Sticker Choices Are Fully Intentional

Students select stickers based on:

  • Emotional attachment (memories, people, culture)
  • Aesthetic preference
  • Control of public self-perception

Sticker selection reflects a balance between sentiment and social presentation, not impulse.

2. Layout Reflects Personal Values

Sticker placement is rarely random. Students described their arrangement strategies using words like:

  • "Playing Tetris"
  • "Linear"
  • "Organized"

Arrangement reveals values related to order, control, creativity, and spatial logic.

3. Stickers Function as Low-Stakes Social Signals

Stickers act as:

  • Conversation starters
  • Signals of approachability
  • Temporary bridges for social interaction

However, students emphasized that symbolic objects cannot fully define identity — deeper understanding comes through conversation.

Qualitative Coding Process

I employed a rigorous multi-cycle coding approach to analyze participant responses:

  • In Vivo Coding: Captured participants' exact language to preserve authentic voice
  • Versus Coding: Identified tensions and contrasts (e.g., "sentimental vs. aesthetic")
  • Values Coding: Revealed underlying beliefs and priorities driving sticker choices

This process generated a codebook with 30+ coded concepts, which were then synthesized through affinity diagramming to reveal broader thematic patterns.

Outcomes & Deliverables

📦 Project Deliverables:

  • 5+ page formal research report
  • Presentation deck with visual synthesis
  • Codebook with 30+ coded concepts
  • Affinity diagram organizing thematic patterns
  • Thematic model of identity curation
  • Qualitative research portfolio artifact

🎓 What I Learned:

  • Everyday objects carry layered meaning: Seemingly casual choices often reflect deep emotional and social considerations
  • Intentionality in design: Even "random" design choices are often intentional when examined closely
  • Mixed-method value: Qualitative research reveals nuance that surveys alone cannot capture
  • Interview depth: Interviews allow deeper insight than questionnaires (a refinement I would apply in future studies)
  • Identity as performance: Identity expression in public spaces is negotiated, curated, and constantly evolving

Research Implications:

This study demonstrates how user research methods can be applied to understand implicit behaviors and identity practices in everyday contexts. The findings suggest that interface design, personalization features, and social platforms should consider how users curate and perform identity through seemingly small choices.
